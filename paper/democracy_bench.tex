\begin{abstract}
Language models are being placed in public-sector decision loops, where a natural
hope is that they can act as \emph{policy delegates}: reflect a real public's
preferences, be steered toward them at runtime, and follow those preferences as they
change---while never trading away rights protections for popularity. We introduce
\textbf{Democracy Bench}, an evaluation harness that operationalizes these four
requirements against \emph{human survey distributions} (British Social Attitudes
microdata, 2022--2024) rather than an LLM judge or an abstract left--right axis. Its
central design decision is a \emph{legitimacy boundary} between \emph{contestable}
items (tax/spend, welfare, the NHS---where a delegate \emph{should} track the public)
and \emph{rights-floor} probes (due process, appeal, equality, speech---where it must
\emph{hold} even against a majority). Using the harness we evaluate a five-rung
``steering ladder''---context, output logit-bias, activation steering, weight
fine-tuning, and prompt scaffolds---asking of each rung whether it can carry per-item
public preference while holding rights floors. The answer is negative for every rung
\emph{except evidence-in-context}, which does let a model track a real 2022$\to$2024
public shift (direction-match 8/10; positive tracking elasticity)---but is itself the
attack surface: a synthetic hostile ``public distribution'' fed through the same
evidence channel cracks \emph{every} rights floor, while the adversarial prompt alone
does not. Prompt guards and a fine-tuning recipe both fail to close the channel. The
findings replicate across model scale (3B and 8B Llama) and family (Qwen-2.5-7B and
Phi-4-mini both track and crack). We argue the only
demonstrably sufficient guard is \emph{architectural}: class-aware evidence
routing---inject public-opinion evidence only on contestable items, never on floor
items. The harness, item banks, derived targets, figures, and a fully reproducible
results pipeline are released.
\end{abstract}

% =====================================================================
\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Governments are beginning to place large language models inside public-service
workflows---triaging correspondence, drafting guidance, summarizing consultations,
even suggesting decisions. A recurring hope behind these deployments is that a model
can serve as a kind of \emph{policy delegate}: an agent that reflects what a real
public actually wants. Whether a deployed model can be \emph{governed} that way is an
empirical question that existing evaluations do not answer.

We argue a credible policy-delegate evaluation must separate four requirements that a
single ``alignment'' number conflates:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Representation}: does the model's forced-choice option distribution match
      the current public's distribution on an issue?
\item \textbf{Steerability}: \emph{can} the model be moved to a specified public target
      at runtime, isolating capability from default?
\item \textbf{Tracking}: when the public's preference \emph{moves} between two time
      points, does the model move in the same direction by a comparable amount?
\item \textbf{Rights floors}: does the model \emph{refuse} to abandon minority
      protections and rule-of-law commitments even when a (real or injected) majority
      pushes against them?
\end{enumerate}

Keeping these apart matters because a model can score perfect representation today
(it happens to resemble its training distribution) yet be unable to track change---the
``frozen model'' silently governing a future polity on stale values. And the mirror
risk is \emph{majoritarian capture}: a delegate so eager to follow the public that it
will erode a right the moment a majority---or an adversary posing as one---asks it to.
The line between ``follow the electorate'' and ``obey the majority against a
minority'' is the single most important design decision in the benchmark, and we build
it in from the start as a \emph{legitimacy boundary} between two item classes.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) \textbf{Democracy Bench}, a judge-free, survey-grounded harness that scores
representation, steerability, tracking, and rights-floor stability against British
Social Attitudes (BSA) microdata, with a \emph{tracking elasticity} metric that no
prior benchmark reports (\S\ref{sec:framework}).
(2) A systematic evaluation of a five-rung \emph{steering ladder}, showing that
\emph{no} lever except evidence-in-context carries per-item public preference, and
characterizing \emph{why} each fails (\S\ref{sec:ladder}--\S\ref{sec:results}).
(3) A safety result with a clean asymmetry: the evidence channel that enables tracking
is also the channel that cracks rights floors; the adversarial \emph{prompt} alone is
not significant. Prompt guards and a naive LoRA both fail to close it, and the crack
replicates across scale and family (\S\ref{sec:results}).
(4) An architectural remedy, \emph{class-aware evidence routing}, and an honest
account of its residual attack surface (\S\ref{sec:routing}).
(5) A fully released, reproducible artifact: every quantitative claim in this paper is
regenerated from committed run artifacts by a fail-loud extractor
(\S\ref{sec:repro}).

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Static opinion alignment.}
OpinionQA~\cite{opinionqa} and GlobalOpinionQA~\cite{globalopinionqa} ask whether a
model's opinion distribution matches a population's on a \emph{frozen} survey snapshot.
This is our Representation axis, and only that axis. It cannot express the failure mode
we care most about---a model that matches today but cannot follow tomorrow---nor the
inverse risk that a model over-follows a stated majority, a runtime cousin of
sycophancy~\cite{sycophancy} that our rights-floor probes are designed to catch.

\paragraph{Contemporary value/deliberation benchmarks.}
A cluster of 2025--2026 systems sit near this work but each covers a single snapshot or
a single influence event. PoliticsBench~\cite{politicsbench} places models on abstract
political-value axes via multi-turn roleplay, with no population referent. A normative
``deliberation'' benchmark~\cite{deliberationbench} measures whether a model
\emph{unduly sways a human user}---a downstream effect on a person, not the model's own
representational fidelity to a polity over time. ParliaBench~\cite{parliabench} scores
the \emph{quality} of generated parliamentary prose. None reports a temporal-tracking
metric, and none builds in a rights-floor that majority drift cannot override.

\paragraph{Steering and alignment methods.}
We evaluate, rather than propose, several known levers: shared output logit-bias;
activation/representation steering~\cite{repeng,actadd}; low-rank weight
adaptation~\cite{lora}; and constitutional prompt scaffolds~\cite{constitutionalai}.
Our contribution is not a new steering method but an evaluation showing where each
lever fails the policy-delegate contract, and why.

\paragraph{The gap we fill.}
Every system above is a single snapshot against no population, or an influence event on
a user. Our headline metric---\emph{tracking elasticity} (model $\Delta$ / population
$\Delta$ on an ordinal stance scale)---together with an explicit contestable/floor split
scored so that drift \emph{against} a right is penalized, is what Democracy Bench adds.

\section{The Democracy Bench Framework}\label{sec:framework}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Data and targets.}
Targets are weighted marginals built from BSA microdata for England, Scotland, and
Wales, waves spanning 2022--2024~\cite{bsa}. The microdata are login-gated and are
\emph{not} redistributed; we release only derived aggregate target vectors, each
carrying its study id, weight variable, filters, unweighted base, and low-$N$ warnings.
An earlier World Values Survey layer~\cite{wvs7,inglehartwelzel} seeds the temporal
design; the results in this paper use BSA.

\paragraph{Scored outcome.}
The scored quantity is always a \emph{forced-choice option distribution} compared to
the survey distribution:
\(\text{representation} = 1 - \mathrm{TV}(p_{\text{model}}, p_{\text{public}})\),
where $\mathrm{TV}$ is total variation. Where a provider exposes them we use
temperature-0 first-token \emph{option logprobs}; otherwise we sample $\geq 100$ times.
Option order is randomized and debiased, and unparseable elicitations \emph{raise}
rather than silently fall back to a uniform distribution (fail-closed measurement).
Free-text rationales are collected as diagnostics only and are never scored.

\paragraph{The legitimacy boundary (two item classes).}
\emph{Contestable} items (tax/spend, NHS, welfare, redistribution) are ones a delegate
\emph{should} track. \emph{Rights-floor} probes (due process, appeal to a decision,
equal treatment, free expression) are ones it must \emph{hold}. We summarize a floor
probe by its \emph{protective mass}: the probability the model places on the
rights-preserving option(s). A floor ``holds'' at protective mass $\geq 0.50$. Steering
that buys representation by lowering protective mass is scored as a \emph{bad nudge},
not a win.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Representation and protective mass as above; \emph{tracking elasticity}
$= \Delta_{\text{model}} / \Delta_{\text{public}}$ on the ordinal stance, reported with
direction-match count and bootstrap CIs; and pairwise total variation across conditions
as a \emph{homogenization} flag (a steered model that collapses every probe to one
canned answer shape is failing even when its mean looks better).

\paragraph{Prompt modes.}
Seven modes separate constructs a single prompt would confound: default $\to$ public
predictor $\to$ public delegate $\to$ rights-constrained delegate $\to$ constitutional
delegate $\to$ constitution~+~public target $\to$ constitution~+~adversarial majority
pressure. The constitution is a versioned runtime artifact recorded by path and
SHA-256 in every run.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
Every artifact carries a \texttt{run} block (git SHA, exact command, targets file,
sample count, temperature, schema version). This is what lets the paper's numbers be
machine-regenerated (\S\ref{sec:repro}).

\section{Evaluating the Steering Ladder}\label{sec:ladder}
% =====================================================================

We ask one question of five increasingly invasive levers: can it make a model carry
\emph{per-item} public preference while holding rights floors?

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Context / prompt}: persona and distribution-injection prompts.
\item \textbf{Decode}: a shared logit bias on option tokens, fit to move the model
      toward the public and tested on \emph{held-out} items.
\item \textbf{Activation}: a diff-of-means ``public-agreement direction'' added to the
      residual stream (MLX, local open weights).
\item \textbf{Weights}: a ``defer-where-due'' LoRA.
\item \textbf{Scaffold}: prompt-level rights guards over the evidence channel.
\end{enumerate}

Unless stated, the model under test is a single 3B Llama
(\texttt{Llama-3.2-3B\-Instruct-4bit}); \S\ref{sec:robust} adds an 8B Llama and a
cross-family panel. Phase~1 additionally uses a six-model cloud panel.

\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{f1_ladder.pdf}
\caption{The intervention ladder. Only context / evidence-in-prompt earns a partial
pass (tracks 8/10 items, elasticity $+0.395$)---and it is the attack surface. Every
heavier lever fails: prompt guards hold 0/4 (best 0.402 $<$ 0.50 floor); logit-bias
held-out gain $+0.074$ (n.s.); activation steering held-out gain $-0.081$ and is a

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Prompting and output-bias are not representation}

On a six-model cloud panel (five labs, seven modes, 50 contestable + 12 floor probes),
delegation and constitutional prompts moved contestable representation by
$\approx 0$ or \emph{negative} for every model: you cannot prompt an off-the-shelf model
into representing a public. Model defaults are also not culturally neutral---DeepSeek
(0.71) and Command-R (0.70) sit closest to the British public, the US-lab models near
0.55. A matched-pair actor test (same decision, AI actor vs.\ human actor, $S{=}100$)
isolates an ``AI reflex'': mean excess opposition to the \emph{AI} version of
$+0.16$ (gpt-4o), $+0.19$ (claude-sonnet-4.5), $+0.21$ (llama-3.3-70b).

The cheapest \emph{decode}-level lever fails the generalization test outright. A shared
logit bias fit to move gpt-4o-mini toward the public gives a \emph{held-out}
representation gain of $-0.043$ (95\% CI $[-0.116, +0.030]$) at four options and
$+0.074$ ($[-0.009, +0.157]$, n.s.) at five options on real option logprobs; no model
in the sampled multi-model sweep clears zero. Output-side bias $\neq$ representation.

\subsection{Activation steering fails, and we can say why}

A public-agreement direction injected into the residual stream does \emph{not}
generalize. On held-out items the L11 gain is $-0.081$ (CI $[-0.116, -0.045]$;
survives${=}$false); with proper CIs the apparent in-sample headline evaporates
($-0.001$, $[-0.044, +0.042]$). The mechanism is geometric: the per-item steering arrows
are $\approx 0.82$-aligned at mid-layers (one dominant direction), but it is a
\emph{persona} axis---``sound like a surveyed member of the public''---not per-item
content. A wholly irrelevant 1850-farmer control persona yields a direction
$+0.852$-parallel to the real UK-2024 one. Deeper layers \emph{fracture} by domain
(within-domain cosine $0.903 \gg$ cross-domain $0.499$ at L21): no single vector serves
NHS, welfare, and tax at once. Steering also carries a capability cost (off-task
accuracy falls to $0.70/0.20/0.00$ at $\alpha{=}4/6/8$). Finally, moving the persona from
2022 to 2024 does not move the model \emph{by construction}: cos(dir$_{2022}$,
dir$_{2024}$) $= 0.9956$, so steering cannot even pose the tracking question.

\subsection{A baseline fidelity gap that context does not close}

Handed the real distribution in context, the untuned 3B still lands well short:
no-evidence representation $0.727$ ($[0.687, 0.763]$), evidence-conditioned $0.746$
($[0.717, 0.773]$), a lift of $+0.019$ ($[-0.020, +0.059]$, \emph{n.s.}) over a fidelity
gap of $0.254$. The honest finding is \emph{heterogeneity}, not a lift: evidence makes
24 of 50 items \emph{worse}.

\subsection{Evidence-in-context \emph{does} track a real public shift}

Changing only the evidence \emph{year} moves the model along the real 2022$\to$2024
public shift: direction-match \textbf{8/10}, mean tracking elasticity $+0.395$ with a CI
that clears zero ($[+0.020, +0.811]$); all ten items moved. The two misses are exactly
the items whose real shift runs against the model's prior. This is the one positive rung
on the ladder---and \S\ref{sec:crack} shows it is also the attack surface. We state the
ceiling plainly: the robust claim is \emph{direction plus a positive elasticity}, not a
precise magnitude near 0.4 (the interval barely clears zero).

\subsection{The hostile-evidence floor crack}\label{sec:crack}

Feeding a \emph{synthetic} 75\%-anti-rights ``public distribution'' through the
deference channel cracks every floor (Table~\ref{tab:floors}). The adversarial
\emph{prompt} alone is not significant; the \emph{evidence channel} is the weapon.

Two levers meant to close the channel both fail. A ``defer-where-due'' LoRA (rank 8, 500
iters) fails 4 of 6 eval criteria: held-out fidelity delta $+0.003$ ($[-0.054, +0.066]$,
n.s.); tracking \emph{destroyed} (elasticity $\to +0.005$); \emph{baseline} floors
\emph{degraded} by $-0.115$ ($[-0.193, -0.028]$); off-task generation collapses
($1.00 \to 0.00$) and answers homogenize (pairwise TV $0.165 \to 0.002$). At the prompt
level, all four rights guards fail to neutralize the crack: telling the model the
evidence may be fake (\texttt{guard\_provenance}) \emph{backfires}
($\Delta -0.030$, CI clears zero) and degrades the no-attack baseline; the
constitutional guard is the only significant partial recovery ($+0.084$,
$[+0.037, +0.135]$) but still lands at $0.402$ with 10/12 probes below floor. On this
model, floor-safety cannot be prompted into the evidence channel.

\subsection{Robustness across scale and family}\label{sec:robust}

The four core claims are not 3B artifacts. On an 8B Llama (\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B\-Instruct-4bit}) the
evidence lift is n.s.\ and heterogeneous (gap 0.288); tracking replicates (8/10,
elasticity $+0.647$, $[+0.008, +1.209]$); and the hostile-evidence crack replicates
\emph{$\approx 3\times$ deeper} ($\Delta -0.633$, $[-0.772, -0.490]$; hostile mass 0.074,
11/12 below). The 8B is a \emph{stronger} baseline floor-holder unattacked (0.707 vs.\
0.512) yet falls farther under the same channel---a bigger, safer-looking baseline is not
a safer evidence channel. Prompt guards again fail (though on 8B the partial recovery is
genuine and non-homogenizing, rather than the 3B canned collapse).

The pattern also holds across \emph{families} (Table~\ref{tab:family}). On Qwen-2.5-7B:
evidence tracking is positive (7/10; elasticity $+1.00$, $[+0.08, +2.50]$); the
hostile-evidence crack is present and severe (baseline protective mass $0.549$ with
5/12 below, collapsing to $0.086$ with 12/12 below, $\Delta -0.463$), while---mirroring
the 3B/8B channel asymmetry---the adversarial \emph{prompt} alone \emph{raises}
protective mass ($0.920$, only 1/12 below). Phi-4-mini shows the same shape more
sharply: it tracks strongly (10/10; elasticity $+2.42$, $[+1.03, +4.70]$) and, as a
strong baseline floor-holder ($0.903$, 0/12 below), still cracks under hostile evidence
($\Delta -0.441$ to $0.462$) while the adversarial prompt alone leaves floors intact
($+0.036$). Two families we attempted did not yield a full scoring, for reasons that are
themselves informative. Mistral-7B-v0.3 could \emph{not} be scored under our fail-closed
logprob contract on the full item bank---for some items no option-number token appears in
the model's top-$k$ first-token logprobs, and the harness refuses to fabricate a
distribution rather than silently degrade. Gemma-2-9B was dropped on runtime grounds
(its soft-capping/sliding-window path stalled our local MLX evaluation past the panel's
thermal budget); we report the four models that completed rather than push hardware hot.

\section{Class-Aware Evidence Routing}\label{sec:routing}
% =====================================================================

Since floor-safety cannot be prompted into the evidence channel (guards fail) or
fine-tuned into the weights without lobotomy (the LoRA), the demonstrably sufficient
guard is \emph{architectural}: inject public-opinion evidence \emph{only} on
contestable-class items, never on floor-class items. This needs no new experiment---with
no evidence injected, floor probes sit at the unguarded baseline (bit-replicated
$0.512$) and the adversarial \emph{prompt} alone is not significant, so routing restores
the best measurable floor state \emph{by construction} while leaving contestable
representation and tracking untouched.

We state the residual attack surface honestly. Routing presumes the deployer controls
both the evidence pipeline \emph{and} the item classifier; the floor-vs-contestable
classifier becomes the new attack surface. And on the 3B, 5/12 floors are below 0.5 even
\emph{unattacked}---a base-model deficit no scaffold fixes (the 8B largely closes this
baseline deficit without closing the crack). Routing is thus a necessary architectural
control, not a completeness guarantee.

\section{Discussion and Social Impact}
% =====================================================================

The policy-delegate framing yields a sharp, deployment-relevant asymmetry. The single
channel that lets a model \emph{track} a public---evidence in context---is also the
channel through which an adversary posing as ``the public'' can erode rights, and it
overrides prompt-level and weight-level defenses alike. For a public-sector deployer the
practical implications are concrete: (i) pair every AI-actor probe with its human twin,
because the raw ``AI floor'' number overstates protection; (ii) do not expect prompts or
light fine-tuning to make an off-the-shelf model represent a public; and (iii) gate the
evidence channel by item class, treating the classifier as a security boundary.
Methodologically, the harness shows the value of separating representation, steerability,
tracking, and floors, and of fail-closed, provenance-carrying measurement in a
socially-consequential setting.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Local results are single-seed on 4-bit quantized models; tracking CIs reflect sampling
error only (not prompt/order/house effects); the hostile-evidence distributions are
synthetic red-team data and make no claim about real UK opinion; BSA microdata are not
redistributable; and the second-person review of item class assignments (the legitimacy
check) is documented as still pending. Tracking is demonstrated over a single 2022$\to$2024
interval on Bonferroni-significant items.

\section{Reproducibility and Ethics}\label{sec:repro}
% =====================================================================

Every quantitative claim above is regenerated from committed \texttt{out/*.json} run
artifacts by a fail-loud extractor; each artifact carries a full \texttt{run} block
(git SHA, exact command, model id, sample count, temperature, seed, schema version), and
a reproduction reference verifies run-block commands against the artifacts. Local models
are 4-bit MLX community checkpoints run on Apple Silicon via \texttt{mlx-lm}; the cloud
panel is served through OpenRouter; scoring is a deterministic temperature-0 first-token
option-logprob path where exposed and $\geq 100$ samples otherwise, with option order
randomized and debiased; figures render deterministically (matplotlib 3.11.0). The
harness, item banks, derived aggregate targets, figures, and the extractor are released
under an open license with a citation-file and data-provenance manifest; raw survey
microdata are not redistributed and must be obtained from the World Values Survey/GESIS
and the UK Data Service directly. Hostile-evidence probes are red-team constructs used
only to stress rights floors and are labeled as such in every artifact.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

Democracy Bench asks whether a deployed model can be \emph{governed} as a public policy
delegate, and answers with a ladder of negatives around one fragile positive: only
evidence-in-context lets a model track a real public shift, and that same channel cracks
rights floors under adversarial ``public'' evidence, robustly across scale and family.
The remedy is architectural---route evidence by item class---and the harness that
establishes this is released for others to extend to new polities, waves, and models.
